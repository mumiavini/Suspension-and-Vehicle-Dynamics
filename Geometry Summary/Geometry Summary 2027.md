 THIS SCRIPT IS STATIC SYNTHESIS ONLY.
 Rates, roll behaviour and the kinematic plots are solved in 3D by
 vdcore.analysis.axle and printed in section 3b of the merged
 summary (scripts/geometry_summary.py). They live there because
 DWSolver needs the tie rod to close the sixth degree of freedom,
 and the tie rod comes from steering_geometry.py -- only the merged
 corner is complete.

 The front-view four-bar that used to produce those tables here was
 blind to dz_lca_mm / dz_uca_mm, so inclining the pivot axes for
 anti-dive left every rate unchanged while the real geometry moved.
 The --legacy flag went with it: it reproduced a known sign error in
 the original script's upright rotation, and a deliberately wrong
 code path has no place in the reference.

 Design camber gain = 57.2958 / FVSA = 57.2958 / 1500
 = 0.0382 deg/mm, the textbook rate for a wheel turning
 about an instant centre 1500 mm away. The SOLVED rate carries
 pivot rake and 3D effects and will differ slightly.

 Values tagged design_intent (chosen, not measured or computed):
   static camber, roll centre heights, FVSA lengths, KPI, roll
   gradient target, chassis stiffness factors, brake bias,
   front anti-dive target, inboard pickup planes.

      has no TTC data. Every force below inherits that assumption and
      none of it is measured.
   2. There is no pushrod in this model. An upright on two A-arms and
      a tie rod has five constraints against six degrees of freedom;
      the sixth member is the pushrod. The solve closes the system by
      treating the UCA as a two-force member with no X component and
      enforcing only the moment about the roll axis, and it places the
      front ball joints at zero caster, so braking generates no
      kingpin moment and the tie rod carries nothing.

   These are screening numbers. They are NOT a load set to size tubes
   from, and they do not cover buckling of the long swept rear legs
   flagged in section 5.
